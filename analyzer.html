How Chinese speakers “hear” a phone number

Chinese has many words that sound alike. When a number is read aloud, each digit echoes a word: 8 (bā) echoes 发 fā, to prosper; 9 (jiǔ) echoes 久, long-lasting; 6 (liù) echoes 流, smooth; and 4 (sì) echoes 死, death. In phone numbers, 1 is usually spoken yāo (幺), which sounds like 要 — “will / want”.

Strings form phrases: 168 一路发 “prosper all the way”, 518 我要发 “I will prosper”, 1314 一生一世 “for a lifetime”. Other strings are avoided: 14 要死, 748 去死吧.

What makes a number valuable?

  • The tail. The last 4 digits are what people remember and repeat.
  • Repetition. AAA, AAAA and longer runs (“leopard” numbers, 豹子号) are the main price driver.
  • The digit. Runs of 8, 9 and 6 outprice runs of 3, 5 or 7.
  • No 4s. A single 4 can cut resale demand sharply.

Frequently asked questions

How is the score calculated?

We start at 50, then add or subtract points for each digit (8, 9, 6 positive; 4 strongly negative), for 40+ known combinations such as 168 or 14, and for patterns like AAA tails or ABAB. Digits near the end weigh more because they are what people remember. The full method is on our About page.

Does the country code matter?

Enter the number the way people will see and say it. Most people read the last 4–8 digits, so those carry the most weight.

Is 4 always bad?

4 (sì) sounds like 死 (death) and is widely avoided, especially at the end of a number. Some combinations soften it — 54 can read as 无事 'no trouble' — but most buyers still pay less for numbers containing 4.

Is my number stored?

No. The analysis runs entirely in your browser. Only if you choose to request an emailed report do we receive the number you typed.
