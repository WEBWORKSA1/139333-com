Staying connected in China: SIM, eSIM or roaming?

Visiting, studying or doing business in China? Here's how to choose — and how to pick a number you'll actually like.

Checklist before you buy

  • Is your phone unlocked and eSIM-capable?
  • How many GB will you really use? Maps, messaging and video calls add up fast.
  • Do you need a local number for payment apps, ride-hailing or deliveries? Then add a local SIM.
  • Bring your passport to any carrier store.

Picking your number

Carrier staff can usually show you available numbers. Run your favourites through the analyzer first — avoiding a 4 at the end costs nothing and matters to Chinese contacts.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a passport to get a Chinese SIM card?

Yes. Mainland China requires real-name registration for mobile numbers, so foreigners register with their passport in a carrier store.

Will my apps work on a travel eSIM?

Travel eSIMs sold abroad typically route data through the provider's overseas network, which is why many travellers report that their usual apps keep working. Check each provider's current terms before buying — rules and routing can change.

Can I pick a lucky number when I get a local SIM?

Often yes — carrier stores usually show a list of available numbers. Numbers with no 4s cost the same; premium patterns may carry deposits or minimum plans.
