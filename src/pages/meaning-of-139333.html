What does 139333 mean?

Six digits, one wish: 要生久 · 生生生 — long life and endless growth. Here is how the reading works, where it comes from and where it falls short.

Digit by digit

  • 1 — 一 / 幺 (yāo). In phone numbers, Mandarin speakers say 1 as “yāo”, which sounds like 要 — “want / will”. That is why people say “yāo-sān-jiǔ” for 139.
  • 3 — 三 (sān / saam¹). In Cantonese, saam¹ is close to 生 (saang¹) — life, birth, growth. In Mandarin the link is weaker, and 3 can also suggest 散 (scatter). So 3 is lucky, but less universally than 8, 6 or 9.
  • 9 — 九 (jiǔ). Sounds like 久, long-lasting. It was the emperor's number and is a favourite for weddings.
  • 333 — 生生生. Sellers of lucky numbers market triple-3 as “growth, growth, growth” or 升升升 “rising”. These are sales readings, not fixed idioms.
Put together: 139 → 要生久 “want a long life”, and 333 → 生生生 “growth upon growth”. It is the same kind of folk reading as 1314 → 一生一世 (“for a lifetime”).

Why 139 is a famous prefix

139 was China Mobile's first GSM prefix, launched in 1994, followed by 138 (1997) and 135–137 (1999). Early 139 numbers carry a status as business and early-adopter lines. 13933xxxxxx is a live mobile number range, so 139333 appears at the start of real numbers.

How 139333 scores

As a domain name

All one million six-digit .com names were registered by November 2015 during a wave of Chinese buying. Buyers prefer names with no 0 or 4 (only 262,144 such combinations), triple digits and phone-prefix starts. 139333 meets all three, which puts it in the better tier of the “6N” market.
