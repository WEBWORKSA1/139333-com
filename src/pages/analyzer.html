How Chinese speakers “hear” a phone number

Chinese has many words that sound alike. When a number is read aloud, each digit echoes a word: 8 (bā) echoes 发 fā, to prosper; 9 (jiǔ) echoes 久, long-lasting; 6 (liù) echoes 流, smooth; and 4 (sì) echoes 死, death. In phone numbers, 1 is usually spoken yāo (幺), which sounds like 要 — “will / want”.

Strings form phrases: 168 一路发 “prosper all the way”, 518 我要发 “I will prosper”, 1314 一生一世 “for a lifetime”. Other strings are avoided: 14 要死, 748 去死吧.

What makes a number valuable?

  • The tail. The last 4 digits are what people remember and repeat.
  • Repetition. AAA, AAAA and longer runs (“leopard” numbers, 豹子号) are the main price driver.
  • The digit. Runs of 8, 9 and 6 outprice runs of 3, 5 or 7.
  • No 4s. A single 4 can cut resale demand sharply.
